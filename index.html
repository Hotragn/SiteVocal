      Frequently asked questions

      What is SiteVocal?

      SiteVocal is a free, open-source website-to-speech reader. Paste any URL (or click the Chrome extension) and it strips the ads and clutter, extracts the article, and reads it aloud like a radio broadcast using your browser’s built-in Web Speech API, with a live teleprompter, a section outline and a resume library. It needs no API keys, accounts or servers.

      What is Site Briefing mode?

      A 60 to 90 second spoken overview of a company or website, instead of every word. Paste a homepage, pricing or about page (or click the extension on one) and SiteVocal reads who they are, what they provide, pricing and audience, and the key questions from their FAQ, straight from the page with no API and no AI service. Menus, buttons, cookie banners and calls to action are never read, and one click on Full Page Read switches to the word-for-word broadcast, each mode keeping its own place.

      Is SiteVocal really free and private?

      Yes. SiteVocal has no backend and your library is saved in your browser’s localStorage. With an on-device voice nothing about the article leaves your computer, and the player’s privacy panel says “0 bytes sent to the cloud”. Some of the most natural voices (Google and Microsoft “Online” voices) are cloud voices run by your browser vendor: the player turns amber when one is in use and switches to an on-device voice in one click. In URL mode the page is fetched directly or through free public CORS proxies, and the r.jina.ai reader is asked only for pages that build themselves with JavaScript; they see only its address. GitHub links go to GitHub’s own API, and the Chrome extension reads the open tab locally.

      How does the Ad-Shield remove ads without deleting the article?

      Before Mozilla Readability runs, stage 1 removes known ad containers (data-ad attributes, .ad-slot, .taboola, .outbrain…) and elements whose class or id contains ad, ads, advert, sponsor or promo as a whole word — so “header”, “read”, “load” and “shadow” never match. Stage 2 removes short “Read more:” and “Related:” link boxes, affiliate disclaimers and newsletter sign-ups. A node is only removed when it is small or mostly links, never when it holds the article. Stage 3 counts everything removed and shows it in the reader’s filter bar.

      How does the SiteVocal Chrome extension work?

      Download the repository, open chrome://extensions with Developer mode on, choose Load unpacked and select the folder that contains manifest.json. Click the SiteVocal toolbar icon on any article: the side panel opens, reads the active tab’s live DOM (including pages you are logged into), picks up the site’s logo and colors, and starts playing. Right-click to start from a paragraph or speak a selection; single-page apps and infinite scroll are detected and can be re-synced without stopping the audio.

      Why do other readers stop speaking after about 15 seconds in Chrome?

      Chrome’s network voices silently stop long utterances after roughly 15 seconds. SiteVocal splits text into sentence-sized chunks of about ten seconds and queues them one at a time with a cancellation token, so long articles never cut off and changing speed or jumping never causes overlapping voices.

      Which voice does SiteVocal use?

      SiteVocal scans speechSynthesis.getVoices() and automatically selects the most natural voice for the article’s language, prioritizing voices labelled Natural, Online, Neural, Premium, Google or Siri. You can switch voices and speed (0.75x to 2x) at any time, and teach it pronunciations in the Docs & Lexicon tab.

      Which websites work with SiteVocal?

      Most pages work: news sites, blogs, documentation, Wikipedia, Paul Graham essays, Hacker News, GitHub repositories, files, issues and discussions, and company homepages built with JavaScript (Next.js, React). Wikipedia, Hacker News and GitHub are read through their official APIs. Other pages are fetched directly and through public CORS proxies, and the keyless r.jina.ai reader renders the ones that build themselves with JavaScript. Sites that block all of them can be read with the Chrome extension or by pasting the text.

              How the 3-stage parser works

              Stage 1
              Ad-Shield. Removes [data-ad], [data-ad-unit], [aria-label*="sponsored" i], .ad-slot, .taboola, .outbrain and friends, plus any class or id token that is exactly an ad word:
              (^|[-_\s])(ad|ads|advert|sponsor|promo)([-_\s]|$)
              Stage 2
              Clutter sweep. Short “Read more:” / “Related:” link boxes, affiliate disclaimers and newsletter sign-ups. Nodes are removed only when small or mostly links — never the <article>, the <h1> or real prose.
              Stage 3
              Readability + telemetry. Mozilla Readability isolates the article, the synthesiser writes the broadcast script, and the filter bar reports DOM Cleaned · X removed.

              Site Briefing: what a website or company does

              Homepages, /pricing and /about pages and marketing landing pages open as a 60–90 second briefing instead of a word-for-word read. On any page, Site Briefing and Full Page Read switch in one click and each keeps its own place. The briefing is read from the page itself, before the Ad-Shield and Readability run: no API, no AI service.

              1. 01 · What They Do

                JSON-LD Organization, WebSite, SoftwareApplication, Product or LocalBusiness, the meta and Open Graph descriptions, and the hero headline with its lead paragraph.

              2. 02 · What They Provide

                Feature cards and sections (each h2 or h3 with its own paragraph or list items) or a feature list, de-duplicated: three to six of them.

              3. 03 · Pricing & Audience

                Plan cards and prices such as “$12 per user per month”, free tiers and trials, open source, menu prices and “Built for …” statements.

              4. 04 · Key Takeaways

                Questions and answers from the FAQ (JSON-LD FAQPage or the page’s own), otherwise each section’s first sentence.

              Never read: menus, page headers and footers, forms and buttons, cookie banners, testimonials and calls to action such as “Sign up”, “Book a demo” or “Start free trial”.

              Summarize Page: the whole page, tables included

              On any website or open tab, ✨ Summarize Page beside Full Page Read and Site Briefing plays a summary of the entire page from top to bottom — never just the first paragraph — in about a minute (up to three for a very long read). It runs after the 3-stage Ad-Shield, so ads, pop-ups and promo boxes never reach it, and it needs no API key.

              1. 01 · Overview

                The page’s own description, in whole sentences, without bylines, datelines or calls to action.

              2. 02 · Every section

                Each h2 / h3 section (or the beginning, middle and end of a page without headings) in its strongest sentences: TextRank centrality over TF-IDF, position, numbers and names. Captions, reader call-outs and repeats are dropped.

              3. 03 · Tables & data

                Every meaningful table, pricing or comparison grid, spec list and key list becomes a Summarized Table Card and spoken prose (“Table Summary for Plans: Compares 3 items across Price and Seats…”), even when Skip Tables is on for the full read.

              4. 04 · On-device AI

                When Chrome’s built-in Summarizer is already on your computer, it writes the section points on-device; the built-in summary takes over instantly whenever it is missing, slow or fails.

              What leaves your device

              • On-device voices (macOS, iOS, Windows desktop voices, eSpeak): nothing. The player shows a green Local Engine shield and “0 bytes sent to the cloud”.
              • Online voices (Google voices in Chrome, Microsoft “Online (Natural)” voices in Edge): your browser sends each sentence to that vendor’s speech service. The player turns amber, says so, and offers Use on-device voice.
              • URL mode: a link starts loading as soon as you paste it. Its address goes to the site itself and to the public CORS proxy allorigins.win; the r.jina.ai reader is asked only when the page builds itself with JavaScript or nothing else answers within 0.22 s. The first full answer is used and the others are cancelled. GitHub links go to GitHub’s own API. The extension reads open tabs locally and fetches with its own permissions first.
              • SiteVocal itself: no server, no analytics, no account. Library, clips, queue and dictionary stay in this browser.
              • Turbo Engine (optional, below): only if you enter your own key, and only to the endpoint you typed.
              Turbo Engine (Optional Fish Audio / Custom TTS & Summarizer API) 0 Bytes Sent to Cloud

              SiteVocal never needs a key: your browser’s voices and the built-in summarizer are the default. If you want a cloud neural voice or an LLM-written summary, paste your own key here. With no key entered nothing is ever sent: the gate is checked before every request.
